I added dark mode to my personal site this month and I got to wondering what research had been done
regarding dark mode and accessibility. The handful of articles I dug up on the subject revolve
around visual ergonomics and the concept of _polarity_. This research suggests that positive
polarity is generally advantageous, with negative polarity offering help in particular situations.
First, a few definitions:

<!-- prettier-ignore-start -->
- **Visual ergonomics:** "[T]he multidisciplinary science concerned with understanding human visual
  processes and the interactions between humans and other elements of a system . . . [and]
  optimizing human well-being and overall system performance" ("Visual Ergonomics").
- **Polarity:** Describes whether text is dark on a light background or the reverse (Mathôt and
  Ivanov).
- **Positive polarity:** Dark text on a light background.
- **Negative polarity:** Light text on a dark background.
- **Ocular media**: Transparent substances in the eye light passes through en route to the retina.
{.bulleted-list}

<!-- prettier-ignore-end -->

A 2017 article in [_Applied Ergonomics_](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/applied-ergonomics)
titled
["Effects of ambient illumination, contrast polarity, and letter size on text legibility under glance-like reading"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003687016302459?via%3Dihub)
offered a likely reason for "the positive polarity advantage":

> [E]vidence strongly suggests that the positive polarity advantage arises from the differing levels
> of illumination produced by the two display configurations. (Dobres et al.)

This points to the fact that in light displays, the light background causes the pupil to contract,
which sharpens the overall image. In dark displays, the pupil is more dilated and thus the image may
appear less clear. Interestingly, the authors go on to conclude that the positive polarity advantage
is "only nominally in evidence under bright ambient illumination". This aligns with the reasoning
above, and suggests that light mode may be the most helpful in dark settings.

Insofar as cognitive performance is concerned, a 2026 article in
[_Ergonomics_](https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/terg20) suggested that people perform better
when using light mode rather than dark mode, "regardless of age, gender, or typical usage of modes
in mobile phones" (Gazit et al.).

In some cases, however, dark mode might be beneficial. There have been reports that some readers
with low vision prefer dark mode. A 1985 study led by Gordon Legge observed seven participants with
cloudy ocular media and found that all of them displayed better reading rates with negative
polarity. (Legge et al.)

On the whole, aesthetic appeal alone probably justifies the implementation of dark mode, so long as
the text-to-background contrast ratios remain accessible. Light mode might offer modest functional
benefit in low ambient light, but it does not seem great enough to shun dark mode. Furthermore,
though the research I found was old and of a small sample size, some low-vision groups may benefit
from dark mode. I am finally left with the opinion: if you can, offer both. If you have to pick one,
pick light mode. And always be sure to check your contrast ratios.
